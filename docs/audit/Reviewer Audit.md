| The fixed-action separator is a new QAP decomposition. | Direct novelty risk; unresolved | For fixed action, the map problem is a Koopmans–Beckmann QAP with rank-one (generally asymmetric) flow `w alpha^T`, over a block-preserving subgroup. Enumerating active-node images and solving residual LAPs exploits zeros in one flow factor. The undirected support graph of `F=w alpha^T` has the action support as a vertex cover, hence treewidth at most the support size. The parameter is this explicit cover size, not treewidth alone: Tree QAP is NP-complete even though its flow graph has treewidth one (Christofides & Benavent, [1989](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.37.5.760)); a tree can have a large vertex cover. Sparse-flow QAP prior art already includes exact branch-and-bound with Task Assignment/Gilmore–Lawler bounds for k-tree/arbitrary flow graphs and LAP-based search accelerations (Milis & Magirou, [1995](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/016763779400061A)). The cover argument itself explains the separator: fixing cover images makes every remaining assignment cost unary, so the residual problem is LAP (blockwise LAP under FedORBIT's subgroup); for `F=w alpha^T`, the action support supplies that cover and one of the two unary terms vanishes. The sparse-flow paper's abstract does not establish it uses this same elimination or block restriction, but it is close exact prior art for the structural regime. Exact published-method equivalence and QAP-R1 coverage remain unresolved. |

## Closest-work snapshot (2026-09-26)

For fixed `alpha`, `w^T P^T L P alpha = sum_{i,j} w_i alpha_j L_{P(i),P(j)}` is a Koopmans–Beckmann QAP with rank-one flow `F=w alpha^T`, restricted to FedORBIT's block-preserving subgroup. This is direct overlap with rank-one-flow QAP, though not proof of a novel or non-novel separator. Wang et al.'s publisher abstract calls QAP-R1 a rank-one-cost-matrix problem and reports four IP formulations (three with partial integer data), complexity analysis, and polynomial special cases; the full definition/formulations remain unavailable here. The official SFU repository now provides Jingbo Tian's 2017 thesis ([record](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17540), [PDF](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/sfu_migrate/17540/etd10294_JTian.pdf)). It defines MAP as `A(X)B(X)+C(X)` and writes its rank-one Lawler cost as `q_ijkl=a_ij b_kl`, then develops MILP linearizations and exact/heuristic methods. That is a related rank-one factorization but, under the thesis's displayed assignment-variable indexing, is not the same as FedORBIT's general directed distance matrix multiplied by a rank-one flow matrix. Tian's thesis therefore confirms substantial QAP algorithmic prior art but does not demonstrate that its MAP-specific methods use or subsume active-image enumeration plus residual LAP. The official SFU thesis-defense abstract by Tian identifies rank-one-cost QAP as MAP, favoring that interpretation for QAP-R1; exact equation-level coverage remains open. The authors' public repository contains benchmark instances/solutions, not the main formulations. Milis and Magirou's sparse-flow QAP branch-and-bound with TAP/Gilmore–Lawler bounds is an additional close antecedent. Christofides and Benavent establish that QAP remains NP-complete even when the nonzero flow graph is a tree; low treewidth alone therefore cannot be used as a tractability argument ([1989 paper](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.37.5.760)). FedORBIT's additional rank-one and bounded-vertex-cover structure may permit an `n^{|S|}` active-image elimination, but that elementary XP bound still needs comparison with sparse-flow methods. A 2026 arXiv preprint on exact qubit allocation adds graph-aware reductions, symmetry pruning, GLB/Hahn–Grant bounds and LAP screening. Its public solver documentation says it accepts nonnegative, possibly asymmetric interaction matrices; its published application uses device-distance matrices and the implementation is documented for unrestricted injections. The paper's practical 60-thread runs use heuristic incumbents, whereas its single-thread ablation uses an oracle cutoff and excludes timed-out jobs from geometric means. The mapping depends on direction. Maximizing response maps through `D=C(J-I)-L`; the fixed-action worst-map separator minimizes response, so for symmetric zero-diagonal `L` use `D=C(J-I)+L`, `C >= max(0,-min_offdiag(L))`. An exhaustive-orbit POC verified the nonnegative symmetric zero-diagonal cost and invariant objective shift through 10 nodes. This provides a matched objective subclass, not a solver benchmark: `chpl` is absent, and the public repository has no prescribed run protocol. For directed `L`, the algebraic reduction still holds, but the method's support for directed `D` and validity of its graph-aware reductions on such inputs are not established. Nor is support for FedORBIT's block-preserving subgroup; direct coverage of the full model remains unresolved ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04058), [solver repository](https://github.com/kamerkaya/StaticQubitAllocation)). The repository is source-only and explicitly lacks the paper's run orchestration, machine configuration, raw logs, result ledger, performance table, and prescribed protocol, so its published measurements cannot be reproduced from the repository alone. Kaku and Thompson's general exact-QAP decomposition, seeded graph matching, symmetric rank-one-flow formulations, and robust QAP with uncertain flows are also adjacent. None alone settles the full max-min problem. The outer support master is standard finite-scenario robust LP. No distinct guarantee for its composition has been established.

An accessible adjacent result, Çela et al.'s Product-Block QAP, studies a symmetric product matrix `xx^T` against a block-constant matrix under the full permutation group, with pattern-dependent polynomial/hardness results. Nissfolk et al.'s rank-one-flow reduction likewise assumes symmetric `F=qq^T`; it maps the quadratic term to `y^T D y` for the single permuted vector `y=Xq`. FedORBIT instead permits distinct `w` and `alpha` factors, an arbitrary directed response matrix, and a restricted block-preserving permutation subgroup. These works are not exact matches and do not settle the sparse asymmetric case, but they reinforce that rank-one and block-structured QAPs have a substantial prior literature. Feizollahi and Feyzollahi formulate budgeted-flow robust QAP as `min_X max_f`, with interval uncertainty in individual flow entries. Feizollahi and Modarres (2012) formulate `min_assignment max_location` where interval-uncertain facility coordinates induce correlated, nonlinear uncertainty in the QAP distance matrix. Adelhütte and Liers (2023) give a Γ-counterpart framework that includes a coefficient-uncertain QAP, reformulated using a finite collection of nominal QAPs and reduced further when the flow-deviation and distance matrices are symmetric. Thus “robust QAP,” coordinate/location uncertainty in QAP, and generic robust reformulation using a QAP oracle are not defensible novelty claims. FedORBIT instead has `max_alpha min_P`; after sign reversal this is a robust min-max problem. Its distinction is a continuous sparse action decision with scenarios from a finite subgroup orbit jointly reindexing both axes of a fixed response matrix, rather than a binary assignment decision with interval coefficient/coordinate uncertainty. That structural difference is not shown equivalent by these papers and does not establish a contribution or novelty of the robust-LP/QAP-separator composition. [Çela et al., *Well-solvable cases of the QAP with block-structured matrices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3500); [Feizollahi & Feyzollahi, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orp.2015.06.001); [Feizollahi & Modarres, 2012 author manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/285918289_Robust_quadratic_assignment_problem_with_uncertain_locations); [Adelhütte & Liers, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01688); [author manuscript](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/178255/1/1-s2.0-S2214716015000123-main.pdf).

Heterogeneous-label federated learning is also crowded: FedAlign, private/non-identical label-set work, and CrowdFed already study label mismatch or alignment. A public benchmark with hidden names does not establish why category similarity, shared samples, embeddings, or parameters would be unavailable in a real deployment. No “first” claim is safe.

Robust graph matching is adjacent but asks a different information question. Ameen and Hajek study recovery of a correspondence between two observed correlated graphs under random or adversarial node corruption; robust multimodal graph matching likewise estimates a permutation from paired graph observations with noisy edges. FedORBIT instead treats a finite block-preserving orbit as adversarial uncertainty over one fixed response operator, without supplying two independently observed aligned operators from which to estimate the map. These papers therefore do not establish the same robust-action objective or separator, but they sharpen the application gap: if a deployment has paired graph/response evidence, an estimated-map policy is an allowed-information comparator; if it does not, the study must justify that information restriction. The current synthetic scope has no such paired evidence and makes no deployment claim, so this remains a limitation rather than a newly established application. [Ameen & Hajek, ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ameen24a.html); [Robust Multimodal Graph Matching](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/4925-robust-multimodal-graph-matching-sparse-coding-meets-graph-matching.pdf).
