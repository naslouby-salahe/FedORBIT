# Scientific decisions

The active FedORBIT study is the cold-start information-channel decomposition defined in `docs/FedORBIT_Roadmap.md` and selected under "Selected study" below; its confirmatory evidence is in `outputs/analysis.json` and `results/`. The current implementation checklist is the [active-study Audit Matrix](Audit%20Matrix.md). This document records the scientific interpretation and redesign decisions that matrix cannot make.

## Reconstructed project and conditional candidate

The implemented legacy project attempted to show that a target can choose a useful training curriculum from an anonymous source response matrix when the fine-label correspondence is unknown. It optimizes against every block-preserving bijection, with one bijection acting jointly on both axes of a directed response matrix. Its response objective is $J(\alpha;P)=w^TP^TLP\alpha-c^T\alpha$, and the action is support-limited. The implementation's separator fixes images of active intervention labels and solves residual blockwise linear assignment problems. The old statistical unit was a directed source→target telemetry-domain pair with repeated optimizer seeds; ToN “clients” are modalities, not independent organizations or physical devices.

That applied cross-modality utility claim is superseded. Raw ToN labels and attack taxonomy are public/available in the benchmark, so the imposed map restriction does not establish natural label-map absence, privacy, or organization-level federated benefit. Close work includes FedAlign's label-name/data alignment, heterogeneous/private-label learning, and CrowdFed's category-level similarity matching. No concrete deployment need for excluding names, samples, embeddings, model parameters, or queries has been established. The available real-data candidates therefore do not validate the hidden-map interface.

Robust graph matching is another adjacent line, but its standard setup estimates the unknown correspondence from two observed graphs (including under node corruption) rather than optimizing an action against a finite orbit of one fixed response operator. It is not an exact objective match. It does expose the information-contract question: with paired graph evidence, map estimation must be considered as a policy; without it, the synthetic study can only characterize the stipulated uncertainty set, not claim that a real deployment lacks alignment evidence. [Ameen & Hajek, ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ameen24a.html); [Robust Multimodal Graph Matching](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/4925-robust-multimodal-graph-matching-sparse-coding-meets-graph-matching.pdf).

The least-weak remaining candidate is a controlled study of the finite structured optimization problem $\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_s}\min_{P\in\Pi}J(\alpha;P)$, where the same block-preserving permutation acts jointly on both response-matrix axes. It is not selected as a publishable study because its novelty gate remains uncleared and no valid application is established. If that gate clears, its independent experimental unit would be one generated response-operator instance in a predeclared stratum, with all methods paired on that exact instance. Blocks are mathematical strata, not pseudo-clients. The primary candidate outcome would be positive robust net-action existence and value, with zero-action abstention reported as a completed outcome. No confirmatory inference or final thresholds are registered.

For fixed action $\alpha$,
$$
w^T P^T L P\alpha=\sum_{i,j}w_i\alpha_jL_{P(i),P(j)}
$$
is a Koopmans–Beckmann QAP with flow matrix $F=w\alpha^T$ and directed distance matrix $L$, restricted to block-preserving permutations. The flow is rank one (and generally asymmetric); if $\alpha$ has support $s$, the right flow factor has at most $s$ nonzeros. This is a known QAP structure, so generic QAP is not the relevant novelty comparison. Because `F_ij=w_i alpha_j` can be nonzero only when `j` is in the action support `S`, every edge in the undirected flow-support graph touches `S`; `S` is a vertex cover and the graph has treewidth at most `|S|`. The useful parameter here is the size of this explicit cover, not treewidth by itself: Tree QAP is NP-complete despite flow-graph treewidth one ([Christofides & Benavent, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.37.5.760)), because a tree can have a cover growing with instance size. Targeted primary-source searches for QAP parameterized by flow-graph vertex cover/treewidth surfaced the k-tree sparse-flow literature below but no direct treatment of this cover parameter; search absence is not evidence that none exists. This connects the separator to established sparse-flow QAP work: Milis and Magirou use a Task Assignment Problem relaxation for k-tree flow graphs and a Gilmore–Lawler bound for arbitrary flow graphs within an exact branch-and-bound method, with linear assignment problems used in the bounds/search ([1995 paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/016763779400061A)). Kaku and Thompson's general exact-QAP branch-and-bound also decomposes into LAP subproblems—`n^2` LAPs of size `n−1` to form lower bounds—so “QAP decomposition with LAPs” is established more broadly. Their abstract does not claim the same support-cover elimination or FedORBIT's block-preserving subgroup ([1986 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-2217(86)90303-6)). The reduction itself follows from the cover structure: after fixing the images `phi` of every vertex in a flow-graph vertex cover `S`, each remaining vertex `u` has only unary assignment costs `c[u,k](phi) = sum_{j in S} F[u,j] L[k,phi(j)] + sum_{i in S} F[i,u] L[phi(i),k]`; the remaining bijection is an LAP (one LAP per block under FedORBIT's subgroup). For `F=w alpha^T` and `S=supp(alpha)`, the second sum vanishes. Thus active-image enumeration plus residual LAP is the direct exact elimination for this sparse-QAP structure. Milis and Magirou's abstract does not show that their k-tree/TAP branch-and-bound uses this same elimination or the block subgroup, but their work and the generic cover argument make the sparse-factor mechanism itself a weak novelty claim and sharpen the needed comparison. A separate 2017 process-mapping study also exploits sparse communication graphs through graph partitioning and fast local search; it supports the broader prior-art picture but is application-specific heuristic work, not direct coverage of FedORBIT’s exact block-restricted worst-map solve ([Schulz et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04164)). It is important to distinguish rank-one flow from a rank-one Lawler cost matrix. Wang et al.'s QAP-R1 publisher record calls its object a rank-one cost matrix and reports four IP formulations, complexity analysis, and polynomially solvable cases ([Wang et al., INFORMS Journal on Computing, 2021](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/ijoc.2020.1003)). The official online supplement is accessible and reports benchmark experiments up to size 2,000; for its compact formulations it obtains coefficient bounds by solving min/max LAPs. However, the supplement does not include the paper's defining QAP-R1 equation or formulation details ([official supplement](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/ijoc.2020.1003/suppl_file/ijoc.2020.1003.sm1.pdf)). Official SFU material from QAP-R1 coauthor Tian identifies the rank-one-cost QAP as MAP, favoring a MAP/Lawler-cost interpretation; this remains an inference until the article equation is available. Under that reading, rank-one `L` gives a possible representation-level intersection in FedORBIT's natural flattening, but no equivalence to QAP-R1's assumptions or formulation has been shown. The QAP-R1 results make it especially important to compare the actual formulations before claiming an advantage for active-image-plus-LAP; they do not settle equivalence to FedORBIT's potentially asymmetric rank-one flow or its block-preserving subgroup.

The full 2017 SFU MSc thesis by Jingbo Tian is now available from the official repository ([thesis record](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17540), [PDF](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/sfu_migrate/17540/etd10294_JTian.pdf)). Chapter 1 defines the multiplicative assignment problem as $A(X)B(X)+C(X)$, with each factor linear in the same assignment matrix, and rewrites it as a rank-one Lawler cost matrix $q_{ijkl}=a_{ij}b_{kl}$ (with the diagonal linear term folded into the quadratic coefficients). Chapters 2–3 give MILP linearizations and exact/heuristic algorithms, including a homogeneous-MAP sequence of constrained assignment problems. That factorization is not the same as FedORBIT's standard flow-distance coefficients $F_{ij}L_{kl}$ multiplying assignment variables $x_{i,k}x_{j,l}$: the indices are paired differently. With rows `(i,k)` and columns `(j,l)` for assignment-variable pairs, FedORBIT's lifted cost matrix is $(w\alpha^T)\otimes L$, whose rank is `rank(L)` when both flow factors are nonzero. It is generally not rank one when the directed response matrix has rank greater than one. Thus the thesis is close rank-one-QAP prior art, but does not by itself demonstrate that its MAP-specific formulations contain the sparse-flow separator. If Wang et al. use the thesis's rank-one Lawler cost-matrix definition, FedORBIT's natural assignment-variable coefficient matrix is generically not rank one; this does not exclude an equivalent transformed representation. If they use “rank-one” for the QAP flow matrix, it is a direct rank-one-flow overlap. The official SFU thesis-defense abstract by Tian describes the QAP special case with a rank-one cost matrix as the multiplicative assignment problem (MAP), and Tian later coauthored QAP-R1. Together with the thesis equation, this is strong evidence for an MAP/Lawler-cost interpretation of QAP-R1, but remains an inference because its defining article equation is inaccessible; the repository file format itself does not establish the objective. The full definition and sparse asymmetric/block-restricted comparison remain especially important. A separate rank-one-flow QAP formulation source reduces the symmetric case $F=qq^T$ via $y=Xq$ and repeated-value frequencies ([Nissfolk, *Binary Quadratic Optimization*, ch. 5](https://www.doria.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/125280/nissfolk_otto.pdf?sequence=2)); this is adjacent but does not settle distinct factors $w,\alpha$. Active-image enumeration plus residual LAP remains an exact decomposition of the zero-support structure, not a presumed new general-QAP method. The most plausible distinction is the outer robust optimization over a continuous sparse action and its action-certification regimes, but no contribution from that composition is established. No novelty or polynomial-time claim follows from the separator identity alone.

An accessible Åbo Akademi thesis chapter on the Nissfolk et al. rank-one QAP reformulation gives its reduction only for the symmetric rank-one flow matrix $F=qq^T$, via $y=Xq$, and cites the original 2012 paper ([Nissfolk, *Binary Quadratic Optimization*, ch. 5 and Paper II](https://www.doria.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/125280/nissfolk_otto.pdf?sequence=2)). That confirms the repeated-value/frequency formulation is an adjacent symmetric special case; it does not cover FedORBIT's potentially distinct left/right factors $(w,\alpha)$ by itself. The direct Wang et al. QAP-R1 comparison is still open because its full formulations are inaccessible here.

A separate exact-rank POC now distinguishes whole-objective linearization from residual-LAP decomposition. For fixed integer `w`, sparse `alpha` of support 1 or 2, and integer directed `L`, the QAP objective failed to lie in the space of one LAP objective on each of the tested `(2,2)`, `(2,3)`, `(3,3)`, and unrestricted six-node map groups: appending QAP values increased the assignment-design rank in all eight checks. This refutes a generic single-LAP collapse on those cases, not known specialized QAP linearizations, nor a general theorem. FedORBIT's separator instead fixes active-node images and solves a family of residual LAPs. The distinction narrows one alternative explanation but leaves the sparse-flow QAP/R1 novelty comparison open; see the exact check in `POC Results.md` and its ignored reproducer.

There is a useful but incomplete complexity observation. For a fixed support $S$, the separator visits $N_S=\prod_g(n_g)_{s_g}$ active-image assignments and solves at most one LAP per nonempty residual block per assignment. Since $N_S\le n^{|S|}$ and each dense LAP is polynomial, fixed-support separation is polynomial for constant $|S|$; enumerating all supports up to a fixed budget also remains polynomial, with an exponent depending on that budget. This is an XP-style bound, not an FPT bound in support size. The roadmap's registered $O(N_S\sum_g n_g^3)$ count is only an operational separator-work proxy: it counts high-level LAP calls, not the multiple assignment solves used internally for deterministic tie resolution, and it omits support enumeration, the number and cost of robust-master LP iterations, coefficient bit complexity, and any worst-case bound for the implementation's cut loop. An ellipsoid argument might turn a polynomial exact separation oracle into a polynomial robust LP algorithm for fixed support under rational-input assumptions, but neither this proof nor a formulation-level comparison against sparse-flow QAP branch-and-bound, QAP-R1's sparse-factor special cases, or rank-one-flow methods exists. The exact-sparse implementation must therefore be described as exact when it certifies, with observed work/runtime only; do not state an end-to-end polynomial runtime result yet.

A bounded fixed-action microbenchmark on two generated matrices for each currently configured block shape found exact value agreement with the repository's generic SCIP/McCormick QAP formulation. Median per-call speed ratios ranged from 123x to 483x in favor of active-image-plus-LAP, but absolute sizes were only four to six nodes and separator calls took under 1 ms. This is evidence of a code-level specialization benefit over the generic in-repository model, not a publication result: it does not compare against sparse-flow QAP branch-and-bound/TAP methods or establish end-to-end robust-action scalability. A 2026 arXiv preprint on exact static-qubit allocation combines sparse-interaction graph reductions, assigned-cost filtering, graph symmetry, and conditioned LAP screening around GLB/Hahn–Grant bounds ([Kaya, arXiv:2608.04058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04058)). The public solver documentation accepts nonnegative, possibly asymmetric interaction matrices, while the paper's application uses device-distance matrices and the implementation models unrestricted injections. For FedORBIT's fixed action, `F=w alpha^T` is nonnegative and may be asymmetric, which the public solver documentation accepts. The objective mapping depends on direction: maximizing response can use `D=C(J-I)-L` to obtain a cost-minimization QAP. The fixed-action worst-map separator minimizes response, so for symmetric zero-diagonal `L` use `D=C(J-I)+L`, with `C >= max(0,-min_{i!=j} L_ij)`. This `D` is nonnegative, symmetric, and zero-diagonal, and `Q_D(P)=Q_L(P)+C sum_{i!=j}F_ij`, an assignment-invariant shift. An exhaustive-orbit POC verified this identity through 10 nodes and block orbits up to 14,400 maps. This establishes an algebraically matched symmetric subclass, not a direct solver comparison: `chpl` is not installed here, and the external solver repository has no prescribed run protocol. For directed `L`, the algebraic reduction still holds, but solver support for directed `D`, validity of its graph-aware reductions on such inputs, and support for FedORBIT's block-preserving subgroup are not established. In its experiments, practical 60-thread runs use heuristic incumbents, whereas the single-thread ablation uses an oracle cutoff and excludes timeouts from geometric means. The work therefore strengthens the exact-method prior-art gate without establishing direct coverage of the full model ([solver repository](https://github.com/kamerkaya/StaticQubitAllocation)). The repository is source-only and omits run orchestration, machine configuration, raw logs, a result ledger, performance tables, and a prescribed protocol, limiting independent reproduction of its reported measurements.

For the currently registered maximum of six action coordinates, support two, and a 36-map orbit, there is also a simpler exact full-problem baseline: a monolithic MILP with one scenario inequality per map and binary support indicators `z_i` linked by `0 <= alpha_i <= cap_i z_i`, `sum(z) <= s`. A 24-instance POC across all configured block shapes, both generator labels, and four seeds matched the production robust solver within `1.8e-18`; six instances had positive action and 18 correctly chose zero action. After one warm-up and three timed runs per solver/matrix, within-matrix median runtimes favored the MILP in 23/24 cases; the median production/MILP ratio was 3.92x (range 0.93–27.75x). Timing ratios vary widely across the tiny instances, so this selected POC is not evidence of a general speedup. The result does show that the registered full-orbit instances are small enough for a simple exact MILP and that no end-to-end separator advantage is currently demonstrated. Treat the monolithic MILP as a required exact solver baseline for any future algorithmic performance claim at these sizes; it is not a separate decision-policy contrast and this selected POC is not confirmatory evidence.

The robust outer action does not currently supply an independent algorithmic novelty claim. Once a coordinate support is fixed, the action variables have box and total-budget constraints, the objective is linear, and every admissible correspondence contributes one linear scenario inequality. `run_support_master_lp` solves the restricted LP; `solve_support_master` calls the worst-map separator and adds its scenario. This is a finite-scenario robust LP solved by constraint generation. Thus the present method is a composition of sparse rank-one-flow QAP separation, explicit support enumeration, and a standard robust LP master. Robust-QAP prior art is broader than one formulation: Feizollahi and Feyzollahi (2015) model `min_X max_f` with budgeted interval uncertainty in individual flow entries, then give an exact mixed-integer counterpart and 2-Opt/tabu heuristics. Feizollahi and Modarres (2012) model `min_assignment max_location` where interval-uncertain facility coordinates induce correlated, nonlinear distance-matrix uncertainty; this is location-side matrix uncertainty in a QAP but not a finite group orbit. Adelhütte and Liers (2023) generalize Γ-counterparts to nonlinear discrete programs and explicitly reformulate a coefficient-uncertain `min_X max_C` QAP as a finite family of nominal QAP oracle calls; for symmetric flow-deviation and distance matrices, they exploit index symmetry and further reduce the count to `ceil((n^4-n^3)/4 + 1/2 - Γ/2)+1`. These works rule out novelty claims based merely on robust QAP, matrix/location uncertainty in a QAP, or coupling generic robust uncertainty with a nominal QAP oracle. FedORBIT's `max_alpha min_P` can be sign-flipped into a robust `min_alpha max_P` formulation. Its distinction is instead the continuous support-limited action decision and the finite group orbit that jointly reindexes both axes of a fixed response matrix, rather than a binary assignment exposed to interval flow/coordinate uncertainty. Neither the sign convention nor this structural distinction establishes novelty by itself. None of these references proves equivalence to FedORBIT's full model, and the current robust-LP plus sparse-QAP-separator composition still needs a distinct theorem or guarantee ([Feizollahi & Feyzollahi, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orp.2015.06.001); [Feizollahi & Modarres, 2012 author manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/285918289_Robust_quadratic_assignment_problem_with_uncertain_locations); [Adelhütte & Liers, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01688)). Unless comparison yields a nontrivial theorem/guarantee for the full max-min problem, the current synthetic-only study has no established publishable novelty; there is no validated applied dataset/story in reserve.

The robust-QAP papers in the closest-work list are related but not exact matches: they make a binary assignment decision under uncertain flow coefficients or location coordinates, while FedORBIT chooses a continuous sparse action against a finite permutation orbit of one fixed response matrix. Sign reversal puts both problems in the robust min-max convention; the uncertainty set and decision-variable structure differ. This boundary is not a contribution by itself, and a direct theorem/guarantee comparison remains necessary.

## Current data boundary

The configured raw files are present under the shared `data/raw` symlink. On 2026-09-25 production validation found Windows 10 Host timestamps parseable, Linux Process invalid because one normalized feature vector has `ddos` and `injection`, Network invalid because one has `ddos` and `scanning`, and Edge-IIoTset invalid for event time. Later split inspection found that Windows per-class chronological partitions overlap globally, so parseable timestamps do not establish a future-only evaluation. These findings are reproduced in `docs/.audit/preprocess-2026-09-25.log` and `docs/audit/POC Results.md`. This is a genuine validity blocker for the six-pair primary claim, but does not imply that the only options are to find new files or stop.

Do not resolve contradictions by selecting a label, selecting a timestamp, or using TEST outcomes. A census of every normalized behavioral-feature group found that all-conflict exclusion would remove 91.67% of Linux Process rows and leave only 3 `mitm` rows. That modality is not salvageable by this rule. Network loses 0.973%, disproportionately `scanning` and `ddos` rows. Under the existing per-class split rule, all eight Windows-shared labels retain nonzero counts in train/meta/valid/confirm/test after filtering; the smallest is 104 `mitm` rows in valid. This clears only nonzero support for the current partition, not a precision threshold or temporal validity. A ToN case still requires a pre-outcome chronology decision and cannot support the former six-pair claim alone. Alternative grouping or censoring policies must be pre-outcome and feature/label semantics must not be fabricated.

There is also a design-level temporal-validity issue independent of conflicting labels. Production `assign_duplicate_groups` records each group’s **minimum** member timestamp; `assign_duplicate_groups_chronologically` orders groups by that timestamp and assigns every member to one split according to the group midpoint in the configured row fraction. A feature group with observations after a nominal cutoff can therefore contribute those later observations to TRAIN (or an earlier split). In addition, assignment runs separately for each class, so class-specific split buckets need not share global time boundaries. The Windows probe has no duplicate feature groups, yet its class-wise split ranges overlap globally (Password TRAIN begins later than DDoS TEST). A raw timestamp/label POC then applied component-wise global 55/15/10/10/10 time quantiles to Windows Host and Network without label or feature filtering. In both components, every Windows-shared attack class is absent from at least one global-time bin; Network `scanning`, `dos`, and `injection` appear only in the first bin, and several later campaign labels appear only in the last. This shows that the existing class-wise split manufactures across-split attack support from separate campaign windows; it cannot support an all-class future-holdout estimand. Conflicting-group deletion can only reduce those counts. Exact feature-group crossing mass was not needed to reject that all-class estimand; it would still be needed for a narrower future-time population. The candidate ToN dyad remains deferred.

## Provisional RQ hierarchy and current design choice

The old eight-question catalogue mixes mathematical correctness, mechanism, applied utility, and engineering performance. A more coherent paper would have:

* **Primary RQ:** Across declared blockwise response operators, how do the joint-map robust value and selected sparse action compare with entrywise-rectangular and point-map policies?
* **Secondary RQ:** Under what generated signal/support/heterogeneity conditions does the objective admit a common positive action or positive robust compromise despite an unidentified fine map?
* **Secondary RQ:** How does replacing one jointly acting map with independent entrywise minima change fixed-action and optimized values across the declared operator strata?
* **Diagnostic:** At what supported sizes does active-image enumeration plus blockwise LAP remain exact and tractable against exhaustive truth and generic exact QAP?

## Synthetic hypotheses and falsification rules

These are candidate hypotheses and validity checks, not a locked protocol. Apart from exactness tolerances and generator-construction floors, materiality thresholds, stratum ranges, sample sizes, and multiplicity rules remain unset pending the novelty decision and pre-outcome precision design. In particular, the legacy `0.005` response-unit threshold is not a default claim threshold for differently normalized synthetic strata.

* **H1 — exact separation:** For every small generated instance with exhaustive truth available, exact-sparse returns the same worst-map value and a valid map certificate within the registered absolute tolerance (`1e-9`). Any reproducible mismatch falsifies correctness; a timeout is an unavailable result, never a pass.
* **H2 — joint-map versus rectangular uncertainty:** For every sampled fixed action, compare the exact joint-map minimum with the independent-entry rectangular minimum and report the gap directly. The `jointly_realizable` label only means a common orbit map attains each active coordinate's importance-weighted minimum; a 10-instance POC found positive rectangularization gaps in all ten such cases (`0.00035`–`0.01282`). It is therefore not a zero-gap control. Use the existing constant-within-block-pair common-action family for the structural zero-gap control and the incompatible label's `1e-6` construction floor for its defined positive-gap stratum. This is a deterministic comparison of uncertainty sets, not a causal effect of randomly “destroying coupling.” Separately report whether the fixed-action gap propagates to the optimized action and value; do not infer action utility from gap existence alone. `0.005` is a materiality reference only on cells whose normalization supports that scale. The coupling-destruction shuffle is exploratory only: the exact-orbit POC shows it changes same-block diagonal/off-diagonal values and the admissible-map objective, and even a stratified shuffle changes higher-order orbit geometry.
* **H3 — action certification:** With more than one admissible map, common-action and robust-compromise fixtures test whether a positive robust action exists; report the net objective itself and do not impose an unvalidated positive-effect threshold. A map-dependent action fixture must have distinct map-conditioned maximizing actions; separately report the value of map information, which is `max_P max_alpha J(alpha;P) - max_alpha min_P J(alpha;P)`, not action disagreement. If useful actions occur only with oracle map information across all tested regimes, narrow the action-certification claim.
A necessary point-map control is stronger than the current one-action-per-map pilot: for each nominal map P0, let V(P0)=max_{alpha in A_s} J(alpha;P0), then maximize min_{P in Pi} J(alpha;P) over actions attaining V(P0), and take the best value over P0. This face-complete point-map commitment includes solver tie choices and should be compared with the joint-map robust optimum on each same instance. The deterministic one-action-per-map envelope remains only a lower bound on this comparator. A post-hoc face LP diagnostic covered every point-map face and support for all 19 positive-action matrices in the selected scale-4 `(3,3)`, support-2 stratum. Maximum tie uplift was below `4.8e-8`, with none above `1e-6`; the robust-minus-face gap exceeded `1e-4` for 4/9 jointly-realizable and 6/10 incompatible positive-action instances. The 41 zero-action matrices were not face-evaluated, so no point-policy conclusion is drawn for them. This post-hoc single-stratum result suggests ties did not explain sampled positive-action gaps, but does not estimate a population contrast and is not confirmatory evidence.

* **H4 — ambiguity boundary:** Report how positive-action frequency and robust-objective regret change across map count, response signal, interval width, usable support, heterogeneity, and support budget. No monotonic law is assumed unless it is established independently; unexpected regimes are retained and explained, not averaged away.
* **H5 — operational solver range:** Work counters must equal the active-image/LAP formulas and exactness must hold where truth is available. Compare wall time, memory, timeout rate and certificates on matched objective/domain instances against the monolithic all-map support-indicator MILP, Generic Exact QAP, and specialized sparse-flow QAP branch-and-bound (including graph-aware GLB/Hahn–Grant methods where assumptions fit). Use the largest jointly tractable sizes/supports relevant to the proposed claim; do not reserve closest-method comparisons only for larger cases. Claims stop at the largest size/support actually certified; Dense-CCP results remain heuristic diagnostics.

Correctness/exhaustive checks remain validity evidence, not standalone scientific RQs. Dense fallback quality and systems scalability are secondary only if they support the main mechanism. Confirmation-safety claims should be retained only if a comparator/control and adequate independent replication show a distinct contribution.

## Design alternatives to compare before protocol lock

1. **Narrowed ToN case:** reject Linux as a rescue. A Windows Host↔Network dyad is semantically plausible under the existing candidate ontology. A full filtered Network scan found nonzero current-rule support for all eight Windows-shared labels in each of train/meta/valid/confirm/test (the smallest cell is 104 `mitm` valid rows). The Network exclusions are class-selective, so this is explicitly a selected clean-feature population, not the original benchmark. The current split is still class-wise rather than a global future holdout; filtered support after any strict-time purge remains unverified. The Windows release also exposes machine-state counters; the recent integrity preprint reports that six such counters classify campaign labels strongly, so feature-removal and campaign/time controls are required before treating any utility result as behavioral transfer. Even if these checks pass, there are only two telemetry-domain endpoints and two directions; report pair-specific results as a case study, not six independent comparisons or generalization over clients. The fine-map uncertainty is artificial because the benchmark taxonomy is public; an applied story needs a credible information-interface use case.
2. **Controlled synthetic mechanism + one bounded real case:** synthetic experiments can carry claims about the mathematical mechanism, with real data as a scoped illustration if it survives validity and baseline gates. ## Claim boundary

Potentially defensible if novelty clears: a tractable exact solution to the stated structured objective; a measured boundary between map identifiability and action certifiability; and controlled evidence about when joint correspondence changes the objective or selected action. Independent source inspection confirms the fixed-action separator's algebra under the roadmap's objective: once a single block-preserving bijection's images for every nonzero-action node are fixed, active-active terms are constant and every remaining term is linear in one inactive-node assignment, so the residual minimization separates into blockwise LAPs. This relies on action coordinates outside that support being exactly zero and on all terms using the same correspondence; adding interactions between inactive nodes would invalidate the decomposition. The implementation enumerates the corresponding product of falling factorials and uses LAPs; its work counters match that structure. Algebraically, the fixed-map objective is a Koopmans–Beckmann QAP with rank-one flow `w alpha^T`; sparsity makes one flow factor zero off its support. Active-image enumeration plus residual LAP is an exact decomposition exploiting those zeros. The exact rank-one Lawler overlap and its limits are derived in the theorem-route section below. The symmetric repeated-value `q q^T` QAP result is narrower and does not settle distinct factors or block restrictions. Direct formulation comparison against rank-one-flow algorithms and Wang et al. remains a publication gate. The outer support master adds exact worst-map cuts and fails closed when the cut cap is reached, so a capped run is not a certificate. Its cut-loop behavior and the work-count formula do not establish an end-to-end complexity bound. A fixed-support robust-LP/separation-oracle proof would yield a polynomial bound for constant support and an XP bound after support enumeration, but standard robust-LP plus a potentially known rank-one-flow QAP special case is not novel by composition alone. The action guarantee is conditional on a fixed response operator and its simultaneous replicate bands; it covers admissible map uncertainty, not population, device, time, release, or distribution shift. Unsupported now: broad cross-schema federated learning utility, real organizational deployment, privacy, naturally unavailable fine labels, universal transfer, general robustness, and target curriculum benefit. Since the candidate paper makes no empirical target-utility claim, target-only/Local-SIR and Local-Only are not method comparators under this scope; if any applied utility claim is restored, they become mandatory matched comparators. A point-map commitment and rectangular-hull comparator test the stated mathematical alternatives. Oracle is diagnostic, not deployable.

### Theorem route to test before protocol migration

For a fixed support set `S` with `|S|=s`, the action variables on `S` and an epigraph variable form an LP: each admissible correspondence contributes one linear lower-bound inequality, with the existing coordinate caps and total budget. A separation query at a proposed `(alpha, t)` asks for the correspondence minimizing the fixed-action objective. Fixing the images of active nodes leaves independent blockwise LAP completions, so the oracle examines `∏_g (n_g)_{s_g}` active-image assignments and solves residual LAPs. Under rational input, exact arithmetic, a nonempty bounded action polytope, the stated block-preserving map group, and an explicit valid finite lower bound on the epigraph variable, an ellipsoid argument would therefore give polynomial-time optimization for each fixed support when `s` is constant; enumerating supports up to `s` adds a factor `Σ_{j≤s} binom(m,j)`. The exponent depends on `s`, so this is an XP-style parameter bound, not an FPT result, and it does not prove the floating-point implementation's numerical certificate or a practical runtime advantage.

This derivation is currently a **candidate proof outline**, not a theorem result. Its pieces are standard oracle-based robust LP plus an exact separator that appears to exploit zero entries in the rank-one right factor. A publication-level contribution would need to establish a sharper property of the composition—such as a meaningful approximation/certification guarantee, a stronger parameterized bound, or an informative structural boundary—and check both the full QAP-R1 theorem/formulation text and rank-one-flow algorithms against the sparse asymmetric case. The QAP-R1 comparison has an exact algebraic boundary. With assignment variable `x_(i,k)=1[P(i)=k]`, the fixed-action Lawler coefficient tensor is `q_(i,k),(j,l)=w_i alpha_j L_(k,l)`, equivalently `(w alpha^T) kron L`; its flattened rank is `rank(L)` when `w` and `alpha` are nonzero. Thus rank-one `L` yields a rank-one Lawler tensor, and rank-r `L` yields a sum of r such tensors. This intersects the rank-one-cost class described in Tian's thesis on the rank-one-response subclass, but does not reduce generic `L` to that class. The publisher abstract confirms QAP-R1 formulations and special cases but not its precise convention; symmetric-cost, directed-cost, block-subgroup and algorithm coverage remain unverified against the unavailable article equations. A separate fixed-rank BQP01 theorem does not directly settle the QAP comparison: Punnen et al. optimize over independent unconstrained binary vectors, whereas `vec(X)` here is constrained to a permutation matrix. The cost rank alone therefore transfers no algorithmic guarantee without a rank-preserving reduction that enforces assignment constraints; none has been established. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3736). The symmetric repeated-value theorem alone does not answer that comparison. Otherwise, the valid conclusion is that the current implementation solves a useful instance of standard robust optimization, which is not enough by itself for the proposed synthetic-only paper.

## Final audit-question disposition

1. **What is being discovered?** Under a declared generator, how joint correspondence uncertainty changes the robust value, positive-action existence and chosen action of a sparse response objective.
2. **Why does it matter?** A decision maker might need to act before fine labels are aligned. No real deployment need for this restriction is established, so present value is methodological.
3. **What is novel?** No publishable novelty is established. The fixed-action map problem is a rank-one-flow Koopmans–Beckmann QAP; the outer fixed-support problem is a standard robust LP. The 2017 MAP thesis studies a different rank-one Lawler cost factorization, so it is strong adjacent prior art but not demonstrated direct subsumption. The smallest rescue is a proven new guarantee for the composition or a defensible application with a real information constraint.
4. **What does raw data support?** Benchmark-specific classifiers and descriptive audits of labels, features, timestamps, duplicates, captures and devices where those fields exist.
5. **What does it not support?** Natural hidden label maps, organizational federation, privacy, universal transfer, or an all-class future holdout on current ToN populations. The local Train/Test CSV also has unresolved row-count provenance and no timestamp.
6. **Are experimental units legitimate?** Legacy ToN units are telemetry modalities, not organizations/devices. A synthetic unit can be one independently generated response operator per declared stratum.
7. **Is there leakage?** Existing class-wise chronological partitions overlap globally, and feature groups are assigned by minimum time. The current split cannot estimate global future generalization; no confirmatory result is authorized.
8. **Is there shortcut/confounding risk?** Yes. Windows exposes campaign-state counters; ToN has conflicting feature/label groups; class and time/campaign are entangled.
9. **Strong alternative explanations?** Public label taxonomy, campaign identifiers, repeated traffic, class-wise time windows, artificial alignment, or method gains explained by point-map choice or action infeasibility.
10. **First harsh-reviewer attack?** The fixed-action separator may specialize established sparse-flow QAP methods, with a standard robust LP added, while no application or distinct theorem has been established.
11. **Simplest competing solution?** For the current small orbit, use the monolithic all-map MILP with support-indicator binaries; exhaustive map enumeration plus per-support LPs is another exact option. The monolithic formulation is smaller to state and directly tests whether custom separation is worthwhile. Separately compare decision policies with point-map selection and entrywise rectangular pessimism.
12. **Closest prior work?** Feizollahi and Feyzollahi's budgeted-flow robust QAP, Feizollahi and Modarres' coordinate-uncertain location QAP, and Adelhütte and Liers' Γ-counterpart for coefficient-uncertain QAP are robust-method prior art. The Γ-counterpart explicitly solves robust nonlinear combinatorial programs, including QAP, using polynomially many nominal-program oracle calls and a symmetry-reduced family for symmetric coefficients. These papers use binary assignment decisions with uncertain flow coefficients or uncertain location coordinates; FedORBIT uses a continuous sparse action decision and a finite subgroup orbit as its scenarios. Although the objective can be sign-flipped between max-min and min-max conventions, the uncertainty geometry and decision variable remain different. The location-QAP paper is especially adjacent because coordinate uncertainty induces correlated distance-matrix uncertainty, but it does not study a finite group orbit. Together these works rule out novelty in generic robust QAP, location/matrix uncertainty in QAP, and robust-plus-QAP-oracle composition, while leaving exact equivalence to FedORBIT's uncertainty geometry unresolved. Milis and Magirou's sparse-flow QAP branch-and-bound, Wang et al.'s QAP-R1, and the 2017 Tian MAP thesis are the closest fixed-action algorithmic comparisons; the thesis's product of assignment-linear forms is not the same coefficient factorization as FedORBIT's rank-one-flow QAP. Nissfolk et al.'s accessible reduction assumes symmetric `F=qq^T` and writes the quadratic objective through one permuted vector `y=Xq`; FedORBIT permits distinct factors `w, alpha` and directed `L`, so this is adjacent rather than exact coverage. Kaya's 2026 preprint on exact sparse-qubit-allocation branch-and-bound adds graph-aware pruning, symmetry handling and LAP bounds; its public solver documentation accepts nonnegative asymmetric interaction matrices, but the paper's application uses device-distance matrices and unrestricted injections. For a minimizing worst-map subproblem with symmetric zero-diagonal `L`, `D=C(J-I)+L` is a nonnegative symmetric zero-diagonal cost with the same minimizers up to a map-invariant constant; `D=C(J-I)-L` instead maps response maximization to cost minimization. The paper's application uses undirected device distances; directed-`D` support, graph-bound validity there, and the block-preserving subgroup are not established. Its practical multi-thread runs use heuristic incumbents, while the single-thread ablation uses an oracle cutoff. Standard robust LP covers the outer master. Heterogeneous-label FL and ToN rectification/integrity studies undermine the legacy and data-quality novelty stories.
13. **Required comparisons?** For decision evidence: joint-map robust, face-complete point-policy selector, and rectangular hull, with true-map oracle diagnostic only. For solver claims at the currently registered 4–6-node sizes, add the monolithic all-scenario support-indicator MILP and exhaustive orbit truth; these are exact solver baselines, not different policies.
14. **Required controls?** Zero-gap structure, incompatible entrywise minima, common-action, compromise-only, and map-dependent action fixtures. A coupling-destruction control is optional only after defining its preserved map-relevant invariants; the current implementation is confounded.
15. **Required ablations?** No randomized coupling-removal ablation is needed for the direct uncertainty-set comparison. Vary support, ambiguity, signal and heterogeneity only as tied to the estimands; add a causal matrix intervention only if a separate causal claim is made and its preserved structure is defined.
16. **Necessary metrics?** Worst-map net value, positive-action and abstention rate, paired regrets, value of map information, action disagreement, rectangular coupling gap, exactness/certificates, runtime and solver work.
17. **Low-value current metrics?** Legacy TEST CE and pair-majority utility thresholds do not apply to the candidate. Identity-map regret/disagreement alone cannot establish useful robust action.
18. **Statistics aligned?** They must be paired at the independently generated matrix level, stratified by generator condition. Optimizer seeds are not independent units.
19. **Enough replication?** No locked plan exists. The old seed design is irrelevant; the 120-instance pilot is only a scale check, and the new 30-instance-per-label scale POC is descriptive. Neither sizes boundary strata or action-rate intervals.
20. **Hard gates?** Semantic validity, valid map/action constraints, exactness against exhaustive truth where tractable, truthful failure retention, and passing the novelty gate before protocol lock.
21. **Historical gates to remove?** Legacy 4/6 and 3/6 pair majorities, TEST CE materiality rules, and seed-based `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` thresholds are non-operative. Establish generator-conditional precision/materiality before confirmatory runs.
22. **Can predictable infeasibility be removed?** Yes: do not register ToN cells that fail preprocessing or global-time support; generate synthetic common/compromise/map-dependent fixtures with predeclared construction checks and retain generator failures separately.
23. **Is `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` overused?** Legacy reporting risks conflating inadequate replication with a well-measured null. Candidate outputs must distinguish unresolved, null, conditional, infeasible, abstention and algorithm failure.
24. **Can cheap POCs help?** Yes; completed POCs already exposed split validity, robust-positive boundary behavior, QAP structure and ToN release mismatch. Keep them diagnostic, not confirmatory.

## Selected study (2026-09-26): cold-start collaboration and information channels

Superseding the earlier retirement notes. The POCs above identify an unsaturated, interpretable regime and a design that answers the questions raised for this audit rather than a method-versus-method comparison.

* **Question.** When a new IoT device has only a few benign windows, what does collaboration with other devices actually buy for benign-only anomaly detection, which minimal shared information (per-feature marginal moments, covariance, model weights or rows) delivers it, and when does sharing more hurt?
* **Mechanism.** Local benign-only detectors fail at onboarding chiefly because the local feature normaliser is unstable at small n; a 230-number marginal summary from partners repairs most of that failure; correlation structure and model sharing add little and can harm devices whose benign behaviour is already well characterised or distinct. Simple local normalisers recover a large share of the naive gap, so federation's own contribution is measured net of them.
* **Novelty boundary (rechecked 2026-09-30).** Not a new detector. DÏoT (ICDCS 2019) already federates benign packet-sequence anomaly profiles: gateways sharing the same inferred device type train GRU profiles and aggregate model weights, with the design motivated partly by small local client datasets. Its sequence model and type-grouped FedAvg do not decompose peer-marginal, covariance, and model-weight channels over a held-out target with a 30–1000-window support ladder; its reported low-FPR results also use a different dataset and threshold protocol. WAFL-Autoencoder (MobiHoc 2023) is a closer protocol precedent: devices aggregate neighboring model parameters and anomaly thresholds derived from local validation. Its global-anomaly evaluation uses MNIST image categories, including dirty training with 1% anomalies, rather than network traffic or a held-out device’s benign support-to-future-test protocol. Laridi et al. (arXiv:2410.09284) aggregate reconstruction-error mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis from validation data containing normal and anomalous samples, then select a global threshold through client F1 feedback. This preempts broad claims to federated summary-statistic thresholding, but the calibration requires labeled anomaly feedback and does not study benign-only threshold transfer to later traffic. Neither work compares peer-marginal, covariance, and model-weight channels over a 30–1000-window held-out-target ladder. StatAvg (2025) already shares per-feature means and variances for federated IDS normalization; Asiri et al. (2025) already evaluate N-BaIoT leave-one-device-out federated anomaly detection, with 10,000 benign training examples per device and local min-max normalization; Fed-DTCN (2026) establishes benign-only federated zero-day representation learning; and Yue et al.'s MCDE (published 2026-09-15) is a direct low-FPR unseen-family detector using supervised known-attack evidence plus benign-deviation evidence and held-out benign calibration. Yuan et al.'s CDA-ADN (published 2026-04-02) is especially close on support: it adapts a source-trained unsupervised IoT detector using 10–200 normal target samples to tune domain-specific layers. Thus small benign support alone is not novel; CDA-ADN differs in cross-domain transfer and its deep adaptation method, and does not decompose peer-statistic channels. Its threshold is selected at the source-normal validation 95th percentile, so it does not establish chronological target-benign FPR transfer. Revell et al. (2026) benchmark labeled 3–15-shot unseen-attack classification on UQ-IoT-IDS-2021, including cross-device attack partitions. Wang et al.'s HEM (2026) adds supervised unknown-attack detection: a binary benign/attack network, a known-attack multiclass network, and a KNN distance model, evaluated by holding out each attack type on UQ-IoT-IDS and IoT-NID. Its official code uses one shared benign-week PCAP and attack PCAPs named by source device. The UQ studies preempt broad few-shot/unknown-attack framing, while they differ from benign-only target onboarding, future-benign threshold transfer, and peer-statistic sharing. The remaining distinction is the measured 30–1000-window benign-only cold-start information-channel decomposition against stronger local-normalizer controls, including the marginal/covariance/model-weight comparison and partner-selection deployability gap; DÏoT’s type-conditioned GRU aggregation, the cross-domain adaptation methods, and the attack-shot studies do not evaluate this same peer-statistic channel estimand. This is a narrow empirical contribution, not a method contribution; its publication strength is unresolved and depends on how convincingly the exact decomposition adds knowledge beyond these studies. Sources: [DÏoT paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDCS.2019.00080), [DÏoT full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.07474), [CDA-ADN](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0344009), [Revell paper](https://www.techscience.com/CMES/v147n1/67129/html), [Revell code](https://github.com/trifle19/FSL-IoT-NIDS), [HEM paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.future.2026.108447), [HEM code](https://github.com/trifle19/HEM).
* **Population and claims.** Primary: the nine physical N-BaIoT devices as clients, claims scoped to Mirai/Gafgyt botnet detection on these devices. External/boundary: Gotham2025's eight attack-observing simulated devices with 70 benign-only partners, labelled simulated. No claim about other attacks, deployments, privacy, or federated deep-model training beyond the secondary autoencoder.
* **Kept strict gates only for validity:** exact-duplicate purge between local support and test benign, chronological test third, test data never used for scaling, thresholds or partner selection, paired local/channel evaluation on the same windows, seed derivation, artifact integrity. Removed as gates: minimum class support per client, per-device significance, universal effect sizes; saturated devices are reported as a separate stratum rather than failures.
* **Reviewer objections and answers.** Naive local baseline is a strawman: answered by no-scale and standard-deviation-floor local baselines and net-of-baseline contrasts. Result is just regularisation: shuffled-marginal partner control. Detector is simplistic: secondary autoencoder with federated averaging. Nine devices: population statements use exact paired sign tests and device-cluster bootstrap and are scoped; heterogeneity is a reported result. Simulated external data: labelled as boundary evidence. Attack scope: stated.
