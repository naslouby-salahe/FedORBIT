**Hostile gap statement (revised 2026-09-28).** StatAvg invalidates any broad novelty claim for federated feature-moment aggregation or shared normalization in IDS: it explicitly communicates means and variances and applies pooled normalization on TON-IoT and CIC-IoT-2023. UniFed/TsmoBN is an even closer deep-model precedent: it explicitly targets external unseen FL clients and re-estimates each target's BN mean/variance from its own incoming test batches with an EMA; therefore, unseen-client normalization, target-specific statistics, and broad model-agnostic/deep compatibility claims are not novel. pFedBBN further uses softmax-weighted collaboration from BN mean/variance descriptors with a self-model weight, preempting generic soft partner weighting and local fallback from normalization statistics. The remaining possible distinction is narrower: benign-only anomaly estimation for a held-out/new target device with small support, retaining the local target centre while using leave-target-out peer marginal variances as a diagonal target for OAS covariance regularization, with an explicit reason to borrow partner information rather than estimating target-local BN statistics from test traffic. This is a problem-setting/mechanism combination, not yet an established algorithmic novelty; OASD and multi-target covariance shrinkage are close estimator-family prior art. Laridi et al. (2024) further show federated AE reconstruction-score moment sharing and global threshold selection, but they require labeled normal/anomalous validation and optimize F1 thresholding, unlike benign-only input-scale adaptation. Yang et al. establish risk-sensitive transfer phase transitions and FedKA/COMMUTE address negative-transfer avoidance in their supervised settings; these further preempt broad claims to adaptive collaboration or safe borrowing, while their losses and data contracts differ from this benign-only detector. Zhou et al. already propose conformal exchangeability p-values to decide whether to transfer source instances, preempting generic compatibility-gated transfer claims; C21 also fails to protect in small support even with an oracle peer model. Rossi & Gallo's latent Markov conditional-variance model and recent selective inference for variance changepoints preempt generic regime detection as a novelty claim; they do not provide a new-device transfer-risk rule. The chi-square gate is not a novelty survivor: its calibration assumes Gaussian i.i.d. target rows, contradicted by heavy-tailed/skewed support data, and its underlying lower-tail test is classical. FedCOF remains adjacent (client means recover class covariance under its assumptions) but is not the closest IDS prior; StatAvg and UniFed are. The distinction from those works and covariance shrinkage must be supported by exact equations, same-window comparisons, and adversarial search for held-out-client benign-only scale-risk adaptation, not wording alone.

## Rejected weak-novelty mechanisms (per instructions, explicitly not pursued as the "novel" claim)

- Sample-count weighting alone (`lambda = n/(n+k)` with no uncertainty term) — tested only as an implicit control inside the two candidates below; not proposed as the final mechanism.
- Hand-tuned convex interpolation with a fixed lambda constant — rejected, no principled derivation.
- FedAvg personalization renamed — out of scope; the registered study already tested FedAvg and found small non-significant marginal gain over marginals.
- Fixed n<100 threshold switch — this is structurally what `shared-marginals` already is at the level of "which channel is active"; not novel.
- Plain nearest-neighbor partner selection — already measured and REJECTED by the confirmatory study (deployable-nearest-2 significantly worse than pooling all partners).
- Plain mean/variance sharing with no adaptive mechanism — this is exactly the existing `shared-marginals` baseline; the bar to beat, not a candidate.
- Vanilla empirical Bayes with no new mechanism — tested as Candidate 1 below; found to fail for a diagnosable, mechanism-specific reason (see POC Results).

## Current novelty status

No algorithm has been selected. StatAvg is the closest IDS prior art and rules out claims to having
introduced shared feature moments or universal normalization in federated IDS. OASD and multi-target
covariance shrinkage are the closest estimator-family prior art; they already cover diagonal and
multiple/external shrinkage targets. TRADER now directly preempts broad claims to adaptive multi-source weighting with target-only fallback
and negative-transfer resistance: its source-guided horseshoe prior uses norm-rescaled source coefficient
estimates, cosine-informed Dirichlet weights, and a local-only component, with theoretical safeguards under
sparse linear-regression assumptions. A future method needs to distinguish the estimated object, sampling
model, loss, and no-harm condition precisely. pFedBBN also preempts the broad design pattern of similarity-softmax
peer weights computed from normalization summaries with a nonzero self-model weight, and FedIG further
preempts learned interpolation between source/global normalization statistics and target-instance statistics
for unseen FL domains. TGMA and TGFed also preempt generic unseen-client transferability scoring and
negative-transfer-aware source-model selection/adaptation: TGMA uses label-free transferability matrices
for pseudo-label correction and source initialization, while TGFed uses original/masked prediction
consistency before source-model aggregation and mean-teacher target adaptation. These methods adapt image classifiers using latent BN descriptors rather than
estimating raw-feature benign scales. A possible remaining distinction is using a peer population's
diagonal as the shrinkage target for a held-out physical IoT device's benign-only anomaly detector.
This is not yet a novelty claim: exact same-window comparison shows a near tie to the stronger
shared-marginals baseline, and controlled Gaussian mismatch can reverse the small-sample gain. Robust
peer-SD aggregation also nearly ties but has no stable advantage; its one-vector summary does lower
communication. Target-specific negative-transfer behavior on real hostile peer subsets is untested. Dependence-aware block-bootstrap inference is established for high-dimensional weakly dependent series (Zhang & Cheng); it motivates the next risk-estimation direction but does not provide a direct small-n AUROC borrowing rule. Automatic dependence-adaptive block-length selection is also established for dependent bootstrap methods (Politis & White, 2004, [paper](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Politis_White_2004.pdf)); C15's rough autocorrelation heuristic is not that estimator. C10 draws on related dependent-data block ideas, while C15 uses a rough autocorrelation heuristic; this literature does not supply either method's transfer-risk or AUROC rule. A 2026 covariance-aware
multi-source SURE shrinkage preprint is direct prior art for source-specific risk updates. C47 implemented its sequential equations on Gaussian variance summaries with oracle covariance and found no paired OAS ranking gain over fixed shared marginals; do not claim this update as novel or transfer the mean-estimation theorem to anomaly AUROC. C46 likewise found that robust scale summaries improve scale MSE in selected contamination cases but not detector ranking. The chi-square gate is not a
credible novelty/safety claim because its Gaussian pivot is miscalibrated on heavy tails. Blocked
predictive NLL misses the AUROC benefit. C34 confirms that using the full OAS covariance NLL rather than
featurewise NLL improves over local-only but does not beat shared marginals; Gaussian held-out likelihood
selection itself is established (Zhou et al., 2011), with recent held-out target selection in structured
covariance shrinkage ([2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17111)). Conformal exchangeability tests
for transfer already exist (Zhou et al., 2017), and 2026 federated conformal methods handle client
heterogeneity; the nine-client rank resolution of 1/9 is too coarse for a conventional five or ten percent
abstention rule. C35 shows joint target mean/scale NLL selection also misses shared-marginal AUROC; this
supports a specific loss mismatch rather than only the fixed-center restriction. C36's per-target peer
weighting approaches the baseline at n≥300 but retains a negative device and has no validated risk or
communication advantage; pFedBBN already uses learned normalization-stat collaboration weights. W1-ACAS
preempts generic adaptive conformal p-value/FPR calibration from arbitrary time-series anomaly scores;
CADES provides finite-sample FPR control for a distinct point-process data model. C38's standard split
conformal transfer fails under the N-BaIoT chronology, so these are prior art/diagnostics rather than
deployable guarantees for the current data contract.

**Loss identifiability constraint (C37).** For a fixed target benign law `P0`, the AUROC of score `s`
against attack law `Q` is the `Q`-expectation of the benign percentile transform
`h_s(z)=P0(s(X)<s(z))+0.5P0(s(X)=s(z))`. Thus, if two scores reorder any attack-reachable regions, an unrestricted `Q` can favor either score. A benign-only method cannot guarantee AUROC no-harm over arbitrary attacks unless it preserves the baseline ranking almost surely. This is distinct from the existing perfect-density non-identifiability result (Nalisnick et al.): here the issue is target attack-law absence for a ranking metric. It narrows any safety statement to a specified attack family or a benign FPR objective with valid temporal calibration assumptions.

**C42 target-local HAC risk is established time-series machinery and fails this chronology.** The variance influence series `q_t=(x_t−μ)^2−σ²` has asymptotic variance `LRV(q)/n`; estimating its long-run variance with Andrews' automatic Bartlett HAC bandwidth is a direct standard construction, not a new collaboration rule. On N-BaIoT, this within-window stationary estimate underpredicts later target-block variance risk (median ratios .045/.037/.129/.049 across n=30/100/300/1000; 84–94% of features underpredicted) and the resulting C10 blend trails exact shared marginals by .0585/.0412/.0200/.0129 AUROC. The iid plug-in underpredicts similarly, pointing to slow chronology/nonstationarity rather than only autocorrelation bandwidth. Closest method family: HAC/spectral variance estimation and the corrected Politis–White dependent bootstrap; none supplies an anomaly-ranking-safe collaboration weight. Any target-local bootstrap continuation is a diagnostic of finite-window risk, not a novelty claim.

**C43–C44 automatic block bootstrap does not resolve the transport problem.** The corrected Politis–White circular block-length selector is prior statistical machinery. On N-BaIoT its target-influence bootstrap estimates still underpredict later-block risk (median ratios .033/.073/.104/.029, n=30/100/300/1000) and its C10 blend loses to shared marginals by .0709/.0365/.0133/.0093 AUROC. Controlled simulation shows the distinction: it approaches the realized risk under stationary Gaussian AR(1) as n grows (mean risk ratios .432/.672/.759/.975), but badly misses unconditional risk under a two-regime switching-variance process (.158/.162/.223/.361). This suggests the real-data weakness is temporal regime risk beyond one target window, not a missing HAC or automatic block-length formula. A future CUSUM/risk diagnostic would be an application of established change-point methods; novelty would have to come from a validated cold-start decision and a concrete advantage over shared marginals, neither of which is established.

**C45 variance-influence CUSUM prognostic screen is inconclusive.** A featurewise normalized bridge CUSUM from one n-row target support window has weak, device-unstable association with future chronological block-risk underprediction (median Spearman −.121/−.038/−.038/−.049 at n=30/100/300/1000; positive device medians only 2/9 then 4/9). Even the top-CUSUM feature quintile remains substantially underpredicted. Standard change-point evidence therefore has not supplied a useful cold-start risk gate here; do not treat this diagnostic as a novel method or safety claim.

**C66 peer-correlation target is an established covariance-target family and is not promoted.** The
leave-target-out mean peer correlation matrix is a multi-target covariance shrinkage construction
with a different target shape; interpolating it with identity to restore invertibility does not
create a new adaptation principle. On N-BaIoT its low-support pAUC(.01) gains are attack-family
weighted, have large worst-device losses, reverse at larger support for stronger weights, and cost
28.5x the scalar payload of shared mean/scale under dense triangular encoding. No independent
confirmation or benign-only weight selector exists.

**C67 featurewise log-variance empirical Bayes is standard random-effects shrinkage.** It estimates
between-peer log-variance heterogeneity after subtracting known Gaussian sampling noise, then uses the
usual normal-normal posterior with target sampling variance. The same C64 factorial gives modest mean
ranking gains but negative lower tails and a concrete mismatch failure: zero peer heterogeneity
collapses the prior variance even when the held-out target shifts. This is adjacent to C1/C9/C12 and
requires retaining per-peer variances rather than only an aggregate; it supplies neither a novelty
claim nor a no-harm result.

**C68 confirms the threat-direction limit of peer-correlation shrinkage.** In a paired Gaussian
factorial with exact target/peer correlation match, 75% peer-correlation targeting at n=30 gains
+.03544 standardized pAUC(.01) for sparse-coordinate mean shifts but loses .00186 for mean shifts
along the target's leading covariance eigenvector. As peer correlation mismatch grows, the sparse
benefit approaches zero. This strengthens the mechanism interpretation of C66 but cannot make the
method novel or safe: target covariance does not encode the attack direction, and peer-correlation
shrinkage remains within established multi-target covariance estimation.

**C69–C70 variance-ratio empirical Bayes is established prior art and not a detector candidate.** Kwon & Zhao's 2023 F-EBV estimator directly models independent simultaneous chi-square variance estimates under scale-invariant loss and proves an asymptotic result as the number of parallel problems grows at fixed degrees of freedom ([Biometrika](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asac019)). C69's 115 dependent features and increasing degrees of freedom do not match that regime; it collapses on synthetic and real scale ratios. C70's moment-corrected inverse-gamma/Smyth EB action repairs synthetic scale risk but still trails exact shared scale on all four N-BaIoT supports for mean AUROC and standardized pAUC(.01), with device harms. This is a useful loss/model-mismatch audit, not novel EB methodology or a cold-start ranking result. C10's high weight is also not evidence of calibrated target risk: C41 finds peer block-risk overestimates held-out target risk by median factors 1.20–7.55, while C42–C43 target-local stationary risk estimates underpredict future regime risk. No deployable risk transport rule follows.

**C71–C72 dependence audit narrows, but does not rescue, variance-ratio EB.** C71 independently varies AR(1) time dependence and equicorrelated feature errors; C70's Smyth action can reduce scale risk relative to local sample variances yet lose to the exact peer scale in matched/sparse regimes. On contiguous future benign blocks it beats peer-only scale error but loses to local ratios on average at all tested n, with large device variation. C72 applies standard HAC effective-df estimation to the chi-square variance likelihood; paired detector AUROC/pAUC remain below shared marginals and nearly unchanged from C70. These are combinations of established dependence correction and empirical Bayes, with no new novelty claim, negative-transfer bound, or detector advantage.


**C73 VASH-style mixture prior is direct variance-shrinkage prior art and this first detector screen is negative.** Lu & Stephens (2016) model independent sample variances by scaled chi-square likelihoods and fit a common-mode mixture of inverse-gamma priors by marginal likelihood; the official `vashr` source alternates mixture-proportion EM updates and numerical mode updates. This directly covers flexible variance empirical Bayes and rules out novelty for a generic mixture-prior shrinkage claim ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw483), [official source](https://github.com/mengyin/vashr)). C73's constrained Python port uses 12 fixed geometric shape components and fits only the variance-unimodal/common-mode model; it does not reproduce the official mode-centered shape grid or automatic variance-versus-precision model comparison. It moves 70–81% of feature ratios closer to peer scale and is much worse than C70 on paired N-BaIoT detector metrics. Treat this as a failed implementation screen, not evidence against the full VASH family; continue only with a faithful implementation if it offers a distinct scientific question, not a novelty claim.
