**Hostile gap statement (revised 2026-09-28).** StatAvg invalidates any broad novelty claim for federated feature-moment aggregation or shared normalization in IDS: it explicitly communicates means and variances and applies pooled normalization on TON-IoT and CIC-IoT-2023. UniFed/TsmoBN is an even closer deep-model precedent: it explicitly targets external unseen FL clients and re-estimates each target's BN mean/variance from its own incoming test batches with an EMA; therefore, unseen-client normalization, target-specific statistics, and broad model-agnostic/deep compatibility claims are not novel. pFedBBN further uses softmax-weighted collaboration from BN mean/variance descriptors with a self-model weight, preempting generic soft partner weighting and local fallback from normalization statistics. The remaining possible distinction is narrower: benign-only anomaly estimation for a held-out/new target device with small support, retaining the local target centre while using leave-target-out peer marginal variances as a diagonal target for OAS covariance regularization, with an explicit reason to borrow partner information rather than estimating target-local BN statistics from test traffic. This is a problem-setting/mechanism combination, not yet an established algorithmic novelty; OASD and multi-target covariance shrinkage are close estimator-family prior art. Laridi et al. (2024) further show federated AE reconstruction-score moment sharing and global threshold selection, but they require labeled normal/anomalous validation and optimize F1 thresholding, unlike benign-only input-scale adaptation. Yang et al. establish risk-sensitive transfer phase transitions and FedKA/COMMUTE address negative-transfer avoidance in their supervised settings; these further preempt broad claims to adaptive collaboration or safe borrowing, while their losses and data contracts differ from this benign-only detector. Zhou et al. already propose conformal exchangeability p-values to decide whether to transfer source instances, preempting generic compatibility-gated transfer claims; C21 also fails to protect in small support even with an oracle peer model. Rossi & Gallo's latent Markov conditional-variance model and recent selective inference for variance changepoints preempt generic regime detection as a novelty claim; they do not provide a new-device transfer-risk rule. The chi-square gate is not a novelty survivor: its calibration assumes Gaussian i.i.d. target rows, contradicted by heavy-tailed/skewed support data, and its underlying lower-tail test is classical. FedCOF remains adjacent (client means recover class covariance under its assumptions) but is not the closest IDS prior; StatAvg and UniFed are. The distinction from those works and covariance shrinkage must be supported by exact equations, same-window comparisons, and adversarial search for held-out-client benign-only scale-risk adaptation, not wording alone.

## Rejected weak-novelty mechanisms (per instructions, explicitly not pursued as the "novel" claim)

- Sample-count weighting alone (`lambda = n/(n+k)` with no uncertainty term) — tested only as an implicit control inside the two candidates below; not proposed as the final mechanism.
- Hand-tuned convex interpolation with a fixed lambda constant — rejected, no principled derivation.
- FedAvg personalization renamed — out of scope; the registered study already tested FedAvg and found small non-significant marginal gain over marginals.
- Fixed n<100 threshold switch — this is structurally what `shared-marginals` already is at the level of "which channel is active"; not novel.
- Plain nearest-neighbor partner selection — already measured and REJECTED by the confirmatory study (deployable-nearest-2 significantly worse than pooling all partners).
- Plain mean/variance sharing with no adaptive mechanism — this is exactly the existing `shared-marginals` baseline; the bar to beat, not a candidate.
- Vanilla empirical Bayes with no new mechanism — tested as Candidate 1 below; found to fail for a diagnosable, mechanism-specific reason (see POC Results).

## Current novelty status

No algorithm has been selected. StatAvg is the closest IDS prior art and rules out claims to having
introduced shared feature moments or universal normalization in federated IDS. OASD and multi-target
covariance shrinkage are the closest estimator-family prior art; they already cover diagonal and
multiple/external shrinkage targets. TRADER now directly preempts broad claims to adaptive multi-source weighting with target-only fallback
and negative-transfer resistance: its source-guided horseshoe prior uses norm-rescaled source coefficient
estimates, cosine-informed Dirichlet weights, and a local-only component, with theoretical safeguards under
sparse linear-regression assumptions. A future method needs to distinguish the estimated object, sampling
model, loss, and no-harm condition precisely. pFedBBN also preempts the broad design pattern of similarity-softmax
peer weights computed from normalization summaries with a nonzero self-model weight, and FedIG further
preempts learned interpolation between source/global normalization statistics and target-instance statistics
for unseen FL domains. TGMA and TGFed also preempt generic unseen-client transferability scoring and
negative-transfer-aware source-model selection/adaptation: TGMA uses label-free transferability matrices
for pseudo-label correction and source initialization, while TGFed uses original/masked prediction
consistency before source-model aggregation and mean-teacher target adaptation. These methods adapt image classifiers using latent BN descriptors rather than
estimating raw-feature benign scales. A possible remaining distinction is using a peer population's
diagonal as the shrinkage target for a held-out physical IoT device's benign-only anomaly detector.
This is not yet a novelty claim: exact same-window comparison shows a near tie to the stronger
shared-marginals baseline, and controlled Gaussian mismatch can reverse the small-sample gain. Robust
peer-SD aggregation also nearly ties but has no stable advantage; its one-vector summary does lower
communication. Target-specific negative-transfer behavior on real hostile peer subsets is untested. Dependence-aware block-bootstrap inference is established for high-dimensional weakly dependent series (Zhang & Cheng); it motivates the next risk-estimation direction but does not provide a direct small-n AUROC borrowing rule. Automatic dependence-adaptive block-length selection is also established for dependent bootstrap methods (Politis & White, 2004, [paper](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Politis_White_2004.pdf)); C15's rough autocorrelation heuristic is not that estimator. C10 draws on related dependent-data block ideas, while C15 uses a rough autocorrelation heuristic; this literature does not supply either method's transfer-risk or AUROC rule. A 2026 covariance-aware
multi-source SURE shrinkage preprint is especially relevant to any subsequent safe-transfer rule; its
source-specific risk updates and finite-sample guarantees must be compared directly. The chi-square gate is not a
credible novelty/safety claim because its Gaussian pivot is miscalibrated on heavy tails. Blocked
predictive NLL misses the AUROC benefit. Continue adversarial search against StatAvg, OASD,
multi-target covariance estimators, federated BN/population normalization, and unseen-client
normalization/test-time adaptation.
