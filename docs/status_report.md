# FedORBIT: reconstructed scientific status

**Evidence cut-off:** 2026-10-01 checkout on branch `algorithm` at `7fe9ef0a` (`temp`). This report reconstructs what is present in the worktree, its audit files, persisted outputs and available Git history. It does not add experiments. The detailed historical index remains [`docs/audit/POC Results.md`](audit/POC%20Results.md); this report consolidates the scientific decisions and status needed to orient a new researcher.

## 1. Current project idea

FedORBIT currently asks: **for a held-out IoT device with only a small benign onboarding set, what benefit does collaboration provide to benign-only anomaly detection, which information channel provides it, and can richer sharing or partner selection hurt?** The current study identity is an empirical information-channel decomposition, not a newly proposed detector or federation algorithm. The target of inference is the physical device; the locked primary population comprises nine N-BaIoT devices and Mirai/Gafgyt attack rows. Gotham2025 is a simulated boundary dataset, not physical-device validation.

The registered detector is Gaussian/Mahalanobis-like scoring on 115 N-BaIoT features. The main comparison is local standardisation, unscaled/local scale-floor controls, all-peer shared feature means and standard deviations (marginals), and covariance pooling at partner sample sizes 300 and 3,000. Support sizes are 30, 100, 300 and 1,000 benign windows, with 30 paired support draws per device for the primary Gaussian study. A secondary autoencoder study compares local detection, shared marginals and federated averaging at support 30/100. Partner selection compares all partners, deployable nearest two, random two and oracle nearest two. Support thresholds are cross-fitted from benign support to a nominal 1% false-positive target; this is a target, not a calibration guarantee.

**Identity/history:**

```text
original: anonymous cross-schema source-to-target transfer, sparse actions, and robust optimization over unknown label correspondences (ToN-IoT / QAP)
→ intermediate: dataset-integrity and identity-shortcut alternatives, including ToN feature/label collision audits and app-market silo screening
→ current: held-out-device benign-only cold-start detection and a comparison of peer-information channels (selected 2026-09-26; confirmatory protocol subsequently completed)
```

The old hidden-map/QAP problem is still mathematically implemented and audited in Git history, but its application premise was not established: the benchmark has labels/taxonomy, and no realistic need to hide correspondences or decision utility was found. Its exact separator work and optimization results are not evidence for the current cold-start study.

**Strongest scientific signal:** at support 30, shared marginals beat local-only normalisation controls on AUROC on all nine N-BaIoT devices, with mean differences +0.1034 versus local standardisation, +0.0315 versus local unscaled, and +0.0467 versus a local scale-floor baseline. The effect shrinks with more local support. The equally important counter-signal is that this gain does not deliver the configured nominal 1% future-test FPR: mean test FPR is 19.54% at support 30 and 8.65% at support 1,000 for shared marginals.

**Available data and evidence.** The registered raw inputs are N-BaIoT and Gotham2025, reached via `data/raw` (external/raw data are not committed as ordinary source files). N-BaIoT's nine-device, 115-feature arrays support the completed within-cohort study. Gotham provides only two eligible simulated targets. The workspace also contains screened material such as Edge-IIoTset, ToN-IoT, CIC IoT-DIAD and HITL-IoT; these did not become compatible independent validation cohorts. Mon(IoT)r and PingPong traces have unresolved access/provenance restrictions and were not inspected at payload level. Many external dataset leads are documented but not locally available or censused. See the data limitations in [`docs/audit/POC Results.md`](audit/POC%20Results.md) and [`docs/audit/Reviewer Audit.md`](audit/Reviewer%20Audit.md).

Reusable infrastructure is substantial: typed configuration in [`configs/fedorbit.yaml`](../configs/fedorbit.yaml), data adapters and normalization, Gaussian and autoencoder scorers, deterministic seeds, provenance manifests and artifact validation, experiment runner, paired contrast/statistical synthesis, reporting and CLI. The completed outputs are persisted under ignored `outputs/` and `results/`; the immutable evidence bundle is [`docs/FedORBIT_Results_Frozen.zip`](FedORBIT_Results_Frozen.zip). The software pipeline is considerably more mature than the current publication claim.

## 2. Algorithm / framework status

**Have we identified a genuinely new algorithm or framework yet?**

**NO**

The completed study compares existing detector/scoring mechanisms and data-sharing channels. Shared marginal moments, covariance pooling, autoencoder parameter averaging, partner selection, cross-fitting, OAS covariance shrinkage, empirical Bayes, HAC/ESS corrections, and robust aggregation are established component families. The project has not established a novel mechanism or framework by literature comparison and independent validation. Many replacement POCs were implemented, but the audit explicitly says no new cold-start candidate earned promotion. A named heuristic or composition of existing parts would overstate the current evidence.

There was an earlier exact sparse-QAP separator in the retired project. Its fixed-action subproblem has rank-one flow `w alphaᵀ`; fixing images of active action coordinates leaves blockwise linear assignment completions. The implementation was tested against exact or generic solvers on small instances. But the robust outer problem is standard finite-scenario constraint generation/robust LP composed with a QAP special case; closest sparse-flow QAP and QAP-R1 comparisons are incomplete, and the applied hidden-label information constraint was unsupported. It therefore does not qualify as an established new framework either. Details and caveats are recorded in the QAP sections of [`docs/audit/Scientific Decisions.md`](audit/Scientific%20Decisions.md) and the retired-era POC entries.

## 10. Current scientific position

### What we know

- The registered analysis on nine N-BaIoT physical devices finds that all-peer marginal mean/scale sharing improves AUROC over local baselines at low benign support, and the average gain declines as support increases.
- The same direction and approximate effect size appear with the secondary autoencoder. Federated model averaging adds no reliable improvement over marginal sharing in that test.
- Covariance sharing does not show a dependable increment over shared marginals in the registered contrasts. Deployable nearest-two scale-similarity selection is not useful in the tested form and can be worse than all partners.
- The nominal support-calibrated 1% threshold produces much higher later-benign FPR. Threshold cause is unresolved.
- No tested scale/covariance adaptation candidate has been accepted; there are informative negative results and one MTS-OAS exploratory lead.
- No independent physical dataset currently validates the result.

### What we suspect

- Much of the shared mean/scale AUROC increase may be carried by partner scale rather than partner center, based on C104. That is a mechanism hypothesis only because the ablation reused outcome rows and retained low-FPR losses.
- N-BaIoT support dependence may cause nominal window count to overstate independent evidence, especially at larger supports. Row order has no verified absolute timing, and measured ESS is diagnostic only.
- Peer predictive multi-target OAS may be a useful low-support refinement. Its averages are promising in some support strata, but device tails, shared cohort and close shrinkage prior art make it unaccepted.

### What we do not know

- Whether the narrow channel decomposition provides a sufficiently new and important scientific result beyond the listed literature.
- Whether shared marginal gains persist on independent physical devices, other attack families or different observation schemas.
- Whether the future FPR inflation mainly reflects finite support threshold noise, serial dependence, chronological benign drift, or their combination.
- Whether any deployable partner or scale-selection rule can outperform simple equal-weight all-peer moments with acceptable worst-device/low-FPR behavior.
- Whether the provisional MTS-OAS lead survives external, prospective evaluation and a direct comparison to existing multi-target covariance methods.

### What is currently defensible as a paper contribution

A conservative, cohort-scoped empirical report of the **measured low-benign-support information-channel decomposition**: on nine N-BaIoT devices, shared marginal statistics improve AUROC over the tested local controls, the gain diminishes with local support, richer covariance has no demonstrated reliable increment, naive deployable nearest-two selection can hurt, and the nominal threshold fails to achieve a 1% later-test FPR. State the data, attack-family, device-count and threshold limits prominently. Whether this is enough for a journal remains unsettled.

### What is not currently defensible

- A genuinely new algorithm/framework, new detector, new federated normalization method, or novel scale/covariance estimator.
- “First” broad federated IoT anomaly detection, unseen-device or unseen-family generalization, general few-shot adaptation, deployment readiness, privacy/secure aggregation, or a guaranteed low-FPR claim.
- A causal claim that partner scale alone explains the gain, or that covariance sharing harms generally.
- Generalizing from Gotham's two eligible simulated devices, or treating screened external datasets as validation.
- Promoting the original hidden-map robust QAP as a validated applied contribution, or claiming end-to-end polynomial complexity or practical scalability from small solver POCs.

### Most important unresolved bottleneck

**The project lacks an independently validated and clearly differentiated scientific contribution.** The immediate evidence gap is a second compatible physical cohort, but data alone will not solve the novelty question: a future study would also need a predeclared estimand and a result that adds materially beyond established moment sharing, federated anomaly detection and covariance shrinkage. At present the software and within-cohort experiment are complete; no new algorithm/framework is established.
